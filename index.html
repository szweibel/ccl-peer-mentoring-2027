Library Agentic AI Peer Mentoring Program

Spring 2027 cohort

Steve Zweibel, Graduate Center · Patty McGowan, BMCC

Council of Chief Librarians · November 9, 2026

Spring 2026 pilot

  • 13 librarians and library staff from nine campuses
  • Each participant worked on a problem from their own job
  • Shamiana Pond (Office of Library Services): a dashboard of ten years of ACRL survey data
  • Kristen Fredericksen (Central Office): a web app for shared print retention transfers
  • Mariya Gluzman (Brooklyn College): an accessibility remediation tool where a person reviews each fix
  • Patty McGowan (BMCC): utilities for technical services work

Schedule and workload

  • A 60-minute online session each week, January 28 to May 21
  • Optional office hours and co-working
  • 2 to 3 hours of project work a week
  • Kickoff in January, public showcase in May
  • No programming experience required

Skills

Build

  • Make a working tool for a problem in their own library and share it with colleagues
  • Automate repetitive work, such as cleaning metadata or reconciling reports
  • Turn library data into dashboards, reports, and searchable sites
  • Take on projects they would otherwise hand to IT staff or a vendor

Understand

  • Follow how agentic AI is changing library work and research
  • Explain to students and faculty how researchers use agentic AI and how the technology works
  • Evaluate AI products and vendor claims from hands-on experience
  • Decide when to use an agentic tool for a task

Changes for Spring 2027

  • Participants use Claude Code during the program and get CUNY AI Lab access afterward
  • $1,000 for each participant who completes a project
  • New project areas: archives and special collections, teaching (including one-shot sessions), and research support
  • Patty McGowan, a pilot participant, co-leads
  • Funded by the CUNY AI Innovation Fund

Outcomes

  • Tools and workflows for participants' libraries
  • Research artifacts, such as datasets, digital collections, and analyses of library data
  • Cleaned and enriched metadata for local collections
  • A presentation of each project at the public May showcase
  • Librarians on more campuses who know agentic AI tools from their own work
  • People that colleagues at each campus and across CUNY can consult before starting their own projects

Applications

  • Encourage librarians, archivists and library staff to apply by December 4
  • Each applicant needs a signed letter of support on letterhead from their Department Chair
  • Give them time for the weekly session and project work, January through May
  • Applicants hear back by December 11
